# 8. Results and Discussion

## 8.1 Comparison with Objectives

The main objective of the project was to develop a digital system suitable for small outpatient gynecology clinics, where simplicity, usability, and workflow alignment are more important than enterprise-level complexity. This objective was achieved through a web application that manages patient information, visits, prescriptions, attachments, appointments, billing, templates, and clinic summaries.

Another objective was to reduce repetitive clinical input using reusable medical templates and dynamic workflow adaptation. This was addressed through prescription templates, lab test templates, and medical case templates. The system is designed to support both pregnant and non-pregnant workflows. Due to time constraints, the non-pregnant clinical workflow was completed and validated first, while the pregnancy module was designed as an extension using the real clinic form.

## 8.2 Strengths and Limitations

One of the main strengths of the system is that it avoids unnecessary hospital-oriented complexity and focuses on essential clinic workflows. The reusable templates also improve efficiency by minimizing repeated writing while still allowing the physician to edit content based on each patient case.

The main limitation is that the system was developed within the scope of a graduation project, so testing was mainly functional and workflow-based. Before long-term professional use, the system would need stronger security testing, backup procedures, and wider evaluation with more users and clinic scenarios.

## 8.3 Contribution to Research or Practice

The project contributes to practice by showing how a specialized EMR system can support small clinic work through reusable medical intelligence, including templates and workflow automation. It improves patient record organization, reduces paperwork, and makes clinical information easier to access during consultations.

The project also contributes to applied research by demonstrating that digital healthcare systems can be more effective when they are adapted to the real workflow of the clinic instead of following a general hospital-centered model.
