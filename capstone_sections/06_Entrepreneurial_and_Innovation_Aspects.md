# 6. Entrepreneurial / Innovation Aspects

## 6.1 Market and Research Relevance

The Gynecology Clinic Digitalization System addresses the need for a simple digital solution in small outpatient gynecology clinics. Many small clinics still depend on paper files, handwritten prescriptions, manual appointment tracking, and repeated clinical documentation, which can make patient information difficult to organize, search, and update. Although many EMR systems already exist, they are often designed for hospitals or large medical centers and may include complex features that are not necessary for a small private clinic.

The innovative aspect of the project is its practical workflow-oriented design. The system focuses on patient records, visits, prescriptions, templates, attachments, appointments, and billing, while reducing repetitive clinical writing through reusable prescription templates, lab test templates, and medical case templates. It was also designed to support both pregnant and non-pregnant workflows; due to time constraints, the validated non-pregnant workflow was implemented first, while the pregnancy workflow was planned as a future extension based on the real clinic form.

## 6.2 Commercialization and Scalability

The project has potential for future use in small gynecology clinics that need a focused and affordable digital system. Its value comes from reducing paperwork, improving patient record management, and making clinic tasks easier to organize without adding unnecessary complexity. The system architecture supports future expansion because it is built using separate frontend, backend, and database layers with React TypeScript, Laravel PHP, and MySQL.

Future scalability could include pregnancy tracking, cloud deployment, analytics, appointment reminders, multi-doctor access, and stronger role-based security. These features would allow the system to grow gradually according to clinic needs. However, the current system should be considered a capstone prototype rather than a complete commercial product, so real deployment would require stronger backend protection, more security testing, backup procedures, and further validation with medical professionals.

## 6.3 Ethical and Societal Considerations

Since the system handles medical records, patient privacy is a major ethical concern. Patient data must be stored securely and accessed only by authorized users. Controlled access, secure authentication, and role-based permissions are important for protecting sensitive medical information. Data reliability is also important because incorrect or incomplete records can affect clinical decisions.

The system helps reduce errors through structured forms, required fields, organized patient profiles, and reusable templates, but templates must be reviewed by the physician before being applied to patient records. The societal value of the project is related to improving healthcare organization in small clinics by reducing paperwork, decreasing administrative workload, improving access to patient information, and supporting digital transformation without replacing medical judgment.
